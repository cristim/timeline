01 · Product Vision

What we are building, why it can win against existing products, and what we refuse to build.

Product definition

VIS-1A visual database of everything across timeMVP

The product is not "an interactive history timeline" (a crowded space). It is a time + space knowledge graph rendered through one interface: a zoomable timeline that is always visible, a synchronized map, and an inspector. It spans from the Big Bang, through natural history and all of human history, to the present and on into scientific projections of the future (red-giant Sun, stellar era end, heat death), covering a usable range of more than 100 orders of magnitude of time.

A user can travel: Big Bang → formation of Earth → dinosaurs → Roman Empire → steam engine → WWII → Moon landing → current events → future of Earth → death of the Sun, without leaving the application, and at any point switch from "what happened?" to "where?", "why?", "what existed?", "how big?", or "what happens next?".

VIS-2Four linked dimensionsMVP

Every feature maps onto one of four dimensions over the same entity graph:

  • TIME - when? (zoomable timeline, temporal buckets)
  • SPACE - where? (map, historical geometry, semantic geographic zoom)
  • KNOWLEDGE GRAPH - how is it related? (caused, enabled, part_of, influenced)
  • PROPERTIES - how big / fast / heavy / populous? (measured values with units, dates, uncertainty)

Map and timeline are two views of the same graph, never two separate features.

Differentiators

VIS-3Combined semantic zoom over time AND geographyMVP

The protected core concept. Not "zoom the timeline" but: the information hierarchy changes as the user changes both time scale and geographic scale. Europe+1500–1600 must not show Japanese village events; Kyoto+1500–1600 must show rich local detail; 14 billion visible years shows ~20 cosmic events, one visible day shows individual sub-events. No competitor fully implements the combined version. Detailed in 07.

VIS-4Entities beyond events, claims instead of truthsMVP

The base object is anything with a time and optionally a place: species, cars, aircraft, computers, games, books, papers, patents, ideas, standards, records. Facts are stored as competing sourced claims with uncertainty (see 02). This single model carries history, science, products, and the future without per-category schemas, and is what makes arbitrary comparison (T. rex vs. elephant, Apollo Guidance Computer vs. iPhone, Roman Empire vs. British Empire) possible.

VIS-5"World at this date" snapshotMVP full dashboard V2

Selecting any date builds a snapshot: political map, rulers, active wars, population, technology available (telephone ✓, powered airplane ✗), scientific knowledge state, culture, records at that time (fastest car/aircraft, tallest building, largest city). Dragging the date animates everything. MVP ships the map+timeline snapshot; the full dashboard (tech/science checklists, records) is V2. This is likely the killer interaction.

VIS-6Knowledge date (second slider)V2

The most original feature: separate world date from knowledge-as-of date. Set world date to 66 Ma and knowledge date to 1850, 1900, 1950, 2026, and watch dinosaurs as each era understood them. Same for age of Earth, the universe, continents, diseases. Requires claims to carry publication dates from MVP onward (see DM-5) even though the UI ships in V2.

VIS-7Technology and causality graphsV2

"What enabled this?" (iPhone ← touchscreen ← microprocessor ← transistor ← quantum physics) and "What did this lead to?" (transistor → ICs → CPUs → Internet → AI), rendered as an interactive graph view. Relationship types exist in the MVP data model; the dedicated graph view and curated dependency data are V2.

VIS-8Explicit future timelineMVP (seed set)

The timeline continues past NOW into projections, visually distinct (see FE-7). Every future item carries model + source + confidence + time range. MVP includes a small hand-curated set (~100 items: climate projections, astronomy, cosmology) to prove the mechanic.

Non-goals

  • No hosting of video or full-resolution media; reference YouTube/Commons (licensing + cost).
  • No full text of scientific papers; metadata + links only (see SRC-6).
  • No paywalled browsing. Free exploration is the distribution engine (SEO long-tail pages: /time/1945/berlin, /dinosaurs/cretaceous).
  • No "everyone edits the database" wiki model; contributions are claims through review (see 09).
  • No pretending soft concepts have hard borders: fuzzy cultural/linguistic areas must render differently from treaty borders.

Business model (context, not build scope)

Freemium: free timeline/map/search/entity browsing with limited AI; Pro (€8–12/mo) adds unlimited comparisons, charts, collections, exports, more AI, private timelines. Later: education tier (classrooms, assignments; school pricing €1k–5k/yr), API/data tiers, museum installations, publisher embeds, restrained advertising. Target revenue mix at maturity: consumer ~30%, education ~25%, API/data ~15%, institutions ~10%, ads ~10%, embeds/sponsorship ~10%. Infrastructure is cheap relative to scale (read-heavy, immutable, CDN-cacheable); the real costs are people, data quality, and AI serving (04, 08).
